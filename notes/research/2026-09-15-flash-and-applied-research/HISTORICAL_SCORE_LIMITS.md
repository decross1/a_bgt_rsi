# Fixed 60-day trade-only baseline: interpretation limits

Static review of canonical `bench/applied_trading/trade_only_baseline.py` at SHA-256 `7addeda71a319e8d3670f7b3ab2a1bea4e8a8fd58585a92c858d14709a30935e`. No scores had been published when this note was written. Keep the frozen result, private predictions, and publisher source bindings unchanged during acquisition.

The paired flow/no-flow MSE and directional point estimates average **all 476 eligible one-hour or 473 eligible four-hour validation rows**. `_day_bootstrap` first averages deltas within each of the 20 UTC **reference-hour dates** (`row.day = times[index].date()`), then resamples those 20 **day means with equal weight**. A 23:00 reference hour has a 00:00 decision on the next UTC date but is clustered with the earlier date. Edge dates have fewer eligible hours because of the embargo/end-horizon exclusions, so the reported 95% interval targets an equal-day mean while the headline delta targets an eligible-hour mean. These differ slightly even with perfect data. The interval is a fixed-model, one-contiguous-20-day, 1,000-draw percentile diagnostic: it does not refit ridge coefficients, cover development-window selection or later regimes, or prove a live-trading edge. Four-hour labels from adjacent hourly decisions overlap, including across day boundaries; day clustering does not fully remove that cross-day dependence. Treat an interval barely above zero as weak evidence.

Features use archive hour `index-1`; the decision/reference price uses the **last trade of archive hour `index`**, and labels use later last trades. This avoids direct future-label leakage in the code. The historical ZIP proves venue event times and a complete hourly aggregate, **not when the derived feature or closing reference price was available to a trader**, nor an executable entry/exit quote. The signed-flow feature is a proxy for pressure/adverse selection, not a direct observation of dealer inventory, intention, or strategic choice.

The 30/60 bps round-trip variants are fixed reference-cost scenarios. `reference_net_bps_per_eligible_hour` is a per-signal diagnostic: every positive threshold crossing is scored independently against last-trade returns, with no bid/ask, depth, fills, capital allocation, or overlapping-position inventory accounting. The four-hour figure in particular must never be presented as portfolio P&L or an executable H1 strategy. The result also says nothing about funding/open interest or the separate H4 crowding hypothesis.

If signed flow fails to improve MSE/direction or reference cost diagnostics on the fixed validation calendar, record a negative or inconclusive mechanism proxy result and retain the source-complete dataset. Next month, test whether genuinely prospective closed-hour flow and quoted-liquidity observations distinguish adverse-selection states; do not tune on the same 20 validation days. If flow improves forecasting materially, regard it as **one historical predictive signal** worth replication on a fresh later calendar and independent quote-aware forward paper observations, with abstention/no-trade and matched lagged-momentum exposure baselines. Even a positive reference-cost number cannot support `paper_supported` until decision-time data, postdecision quotes, missing-event denominators, and cost/fill assumptions are independently admitted. An agentic game-theory interpretation additionally needs a separate agent quote/abstention task with explicit adverse-selection and inventory payoffs against a scripted analytical baseline; market correlation alone is not strategic-behavior proof.
